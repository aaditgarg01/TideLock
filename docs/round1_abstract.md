# TideLock: predictive plastic interception under tidal drainage constraints

**InnovateX Theme 3 · First-stage software feasibility prototype · 9 October 2026**

**Problem.** An interceptor that retains floating plastic also adds hydraulic resistance. Basket filling, intense runoff and an elevated downstream tide can therefore turn a pollution-control device into a drainage obstruction. A useful design must explain when to intercept, when to bypass and when gravity drainage cannot preserve an upstream level limit. Mumbai's documented tide-gate and pumping practices make this a locally relevant engineering conflict; this prototype is not calibrated to a real outlet.

**Proposed mechanism.** TideLock models an upstream storage reach, screened collection basket, controllable bypass and separate nonreturn flap. A finite-horizon controller selects bypass actions after rejecting plans that exceed an upstream level cap in sampled uncertainty cases. Basket filling reduces screened-branch conductance. Sensor-quality checks trigger a head-sensitive fallback request and alert. This combination is an application-specific research hypothesis; MPC, automatic trash screens and fault detection are established approaches.

**Prototype and evaluation.** A reproducible Python virtual plant compares a fixed screen, hysteretic threshold control and predictive control under identical input sequences. The pilot comprises 17 synthetic scenarios, five seeds and three controllers: 255 runs. It includes ordinary runoff, compound tide/rain, blockage, a near-full basket, telemetry faults, actuator faults and an extreme tide. Water and plastic ledgers, numerical refinement, risk, capture and computation are recorded.

**Preliminary simulated evidence.** In the compound scenario, mean incoming plastic is 34.15 kg over a synthetic 30-minute event. Predictive control captures 45.1% versus 39.0% for threshold control; both have zero sampled safety-limit violations in those five runs. The fixed screen captures 58.7% but exceeds the limit for an average 559 seconds. Ordinary and high-tide-only cases show no predictive advantage. Near-full-basket and several sensor-fault cases violate the limit even with predictive control. These findings support further investigation, not a claim of field safety or universal superiority.

**Feasibility and impact.** The initial software runs locally without a paid service. Physical cost remains unquoted because geometry, civil works, corrosion protection, access and collection labor dominate credible deployment costing. The next stage is a stronger safety supervisor and independent hydraulic validation, followed by a controlled flume experiment. Any supervised Mumbai pilot needs drainage-operator participation, surveyed levels, runoff and litter measurements, a removal route and approved emergency procedures. Impact would be measured as net plastic delivered to a verified handling chain without worsening drainage, rather than extrapolating synthetic kilograms to citywide savings.
